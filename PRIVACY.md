# Amazanban privacy policy

_Last updated: October 16, 2026_

Amazanban is a Chrome extension that shows your amazon.com orders as a Kanban board. This policy explains what it does with your information. In short: it reads your orders page in your browser to draw the board, and nothing leaves your computer.

## What Amazanban reads

When you open **Your Orders** on amazon.com, Amazanban reads the order information already on that page: item names and pictures, order numbers, order dates, totals, recipient names, and delivery status. To show every order in the time range you pick, it also opens the other pages of your order list in hidden frames inside the same tab and reads them the same way.

Amazanban only runs on amazon.com order history pages. It does not read any other site or any other part of Amazon.

## What Amazanban stores

It saves two preferences in Chrome's local extension storage (`chrome.storage.local`) on your computer:

- whether the board opens automatically, and
- which columns you collapsed.

Your order information is kept in memory only while the page is open. It is not saved.

## What Amazanban sends, sells, or shares

Nothing. Amazanban makes no network requests of its own besides loading your own Amazon order pages from amazon.com, which is what your browser would do if you clicked through them yourself. It has no servers, analytics, ads, or tracking. Your information is never sold, shared with third parties, or used for anything other than drawing the board.

## Your choices

You can turn off **Open by default** in the board's header, or remove the extension from `chrome://extensions` at any time. Removing it deletes its saved preferences.

## Not affiliated with Amazon

Amazanban is an independent project. It is not made, endorsed, or supported by Amazon.com, Inc. or its affiliates.

## Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date. Questions or concerns can go to the project's [GitHub issues](https://github.com/danmunz/amazanban/issues).
